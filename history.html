Team History

From the ashes of Jaguar Racing to multiple world championships – the remarkable rise of Red Bull Racing.

1995–2004 · Red Bull as Sponsor

Red Bull first entered Formula 1 as a sponsor of Sauber. The energy-drink company built brand presence while learning the sport.

November 2004 · The $1 Purchase

Ford put Jaguar Racing up for sale. Red Bull co-founder Dietrich Mateschitz bought the team for a symbolic $1, committing to invest hundreds of millions over the following seasons. The team was rebranded Red Bull Racing for 2005.

2005 · Debut Season

Christian Horner (then 31) was appointed Team Principal – the youngest in F1 at the time. Drivers David Coulthard and Christian Klien scored more points in the first season than Jaguar had managed in its final two years combined.

2006–2008 · Building the Foundation

Ferrari engines in 2006, then a long Renault partnership. Adrian Newey joined as Chief Technical Officer in 2006/07, setting the technical direction that would define the team.

2009 · First Victory

Sebastian Vettel (promoted from sister team Toro Rosso) delivered Red Bull’s maiden pole and win at the Chinese Grand Prix in the RB5. The team finished second in the Constructors’ Championship.

2010–2013 · The Vettel Dynasty

Four consecutive Drivers’ and Constructors’ Championships. Vettel became the youngest World Champion in 2010 and dominated the era with the RB6–RB9 cars. Mark Webber was a key teammate and multiple race winner.

2014–2020 · Hybrid Era Rebuild

New hybrid regulations favoured Mercedes. Red Bull remained competitive with Renault (later TAG Heuer-branded) power. Max Verstappen joined in 2016 and scored his first win on debut at the Spanish Grand Prix – the youngest winner in F1 history.

2019–2021 · Honda Partnership

Full works Honda engines from 2019. In 2021 Verstappen won a dramatic Drivers’ Championship in Abu Dhabi, ending Mercedes’ run of titles.

2022–2023 · Record Dominance

RB18 and especially the RB19 delivered back-to-back Constructors’ titles. Verstappen won 15 races in 2022 and a record 19 of 22 in 2023. Red Bull Powertrains took over Honda’s IP after Honda’s withdrawal.

2024 · Fourth Title for Verstappen

Max claimed his fourth consecutive Drivers’ Championship. The team finished third in Constructors as McLaren and Ferrari closed the gap.

2025 · New Leadership

Christian Horner departed mid-season after 20 years. Laurent Mekies (from sister team Racing Bulls) became Team Principal. Verstappen mounted a strong recovery but finished runner-up.

2026 · New Power Unit Era

Red Bull Ford Powertrains DM01 engine debuts with the RB22. Max Verstappen and Isack Hadjar form the driver line-up under Laurent Mekies.
